1. ONE-WAY, WHICH IS HOW YOU ACTUALLY TRAVEL
=====================================================================

Your notes have said "one-way tickets each leg" since 7 August. Five
builds got made on top of a return assumption anyway. That's fixed in
eight places:

  * A ONE-WAY SWITCH, on the Travel Desk, above the money — because it
    decides the fare, the bags, the last day and whether you can board.
  * The interview now asks "are you coming back, or moving on?"
  * hops() no longer invents a flight home — that was an extra segment
    and an hour of phantom air time on every plan.
  * The true cost no longer adds a return baggage sector. That was
    20kg x 2 you were never going to check.
  * The price journal was querying "return economy airfare" as fixed
    text — so it has been pricing the wrong product. It now asks for
    one-way explicitly, and tells the model NOT to halve a return,
    because one-way is roughly half on the Asian low-cost carriers and
    seventy to ninety per cent of a return on full-service.
  * The report said "return, per person" as fixed text.
  * The last day of a one-way trip is no longer "keep it near the
    airport" — you're not going home, you're moving on, and it's now
    planned as the day to decide the next country.
  * The planner is told not to hit a budget by thinning the trip.


=====================================================================
2. THE ONWARD TICKET
=====================================================================

This is the one that stops people at an airport, and it's specific to
how you travel.

Indonesia, Thailand and the Philippines require proof of onward
travel. It's the AIRLINE that enforces it, at the check-in desk,
because they're liable for carrying someone a country won't admit.
Island nations reject bus and train tickets outright.

Chappy had this as a seven-days-out checklist item that TICKED ITSELF
DONE whenever any flight was booked. Having a flight is not having an
onward ticket, and for you it's almost never the same thing.

Now:

  * A per-country table with how hard each one actually enforces it,
    and whether a land ticket is accepted.
  * YOUR METHOD IS THE DEFAULT. The temporary reservation that
    auto-cancels — real PNR, about 48 hours, a few dollars — is what
    the app assumes and records, not something it explains to you
    every trip.
  * It checks the expiry against your ARRIVAL date. A reservation
    bought three weeks early has already cancelled itself by the time
    you reach the desk, and that is the specific way this goes wrong.
  * Missing onward proof where it's strictly enforced is now a HARD
    CAP on the deal score — 32/100. Same class as the passport rule:
    a plan you can't board isn't a 90.
  * It never offers a generated PDF. That's document fraud, it risks
    an entry ban, and it costs the same as the legal option.

4. WHAT WILL IT BUY  (Travel Desk menu)
=====================================================================

Your $5,000 question, built.

Ask for a trip on a budget and a model told to hit $5,000 will hit
$5,000 — by thinning the trip until it fits. The deal score doesn't
catch it either, because every line sits at the bottom of its band,
which reads as good value.

So this runs BEFORE the planner: budget, minus flights, divided by
nights and party, against what each country actually costs. The costs
come from the ten long-stay simulations rather than a guess.

$5,000, two of you, 60 nights, one-way works out at about $66 a night
on the ground. That fits Cambodia, Laos, Vietnam, Java, Malaysia and
Chiang Mai. It doesn't reach Bali, the Thai islands or Japan.

It advises rather than blocks. It's your money.

5. THE WATCH ACTUALLY RUNS NOW
=====================================================================

  * BACKGROUND REFRESH. It only ever ran when you opened the app, so
    a week without opening it was a week of nothing checked. It now
    hangs off the background task ChappyProactive already owns —
    rather than registering a second one, which iOS would wake less
    often.
  * THE DEAL FEEDS, DAILY — AND CHAPPY DOES THE WORK.
    I Know The Pilot, Beat That Flight and Secret Flying, read once a
    day and matched against your watched routes.

    The first version of this posted a reminder saying "a feed
    mentioned your route" and left you to go and check. That is half
    the job — it moves the work rather than doing it.

    Now, when a feed hit matches a route you're watching, Chappy
    immediately prices that route itself and compares it against your
    own journal. Then:

      CONFIRMED    "BNE -> SGN: $389 — confirmed, cheapest I've seen"
                   with a Book it button that goes straight to
                   Skyscanner for that route, your dates, one-way,
                   two adults. One tap from bookable.

      UNCONFIRMED  It goes on the Flights screen with "I priced the
                   route straight after and couldn't see it — may be
                   sold out, or different dates, or not real. Worth
                   thirty seconds, not more." NO notification.

    You only get buzzed when it held up. A watch that cries wolf gets
    muted inside a fortnight and is then worth nothing.
  * A SALE CALENDAR. Jetstar's Friday Frenzy is weekly and
    predictable; AirAsia, VietJet, Cebu and Scoot all recur. Knowing
    when to look costs nothing.

And the honest ceiling, stated in the app rather than implied away: a
six-hour error fare will not be caught by any scheduled job. Those are
found by people and gone before a weekly check runs. Chappy watches
the feeds that find them. That is real and it is not the same thing.

WHAT THE REVIEW AND THE SIMULATION CAUGHT
=====================================================================

ONE THAT WOULD NOT HAVE BUILT

  * A bare assignment at the top of a @ViewBuilder body:
    `let f = DateFormatter(); f.dateFormat = "..."`. The declaration
    is fine; the assignment is a ()-typed statement and ViewBuilder
    has no Void overload. It was the only one in 13,500 lines.

FOUND BY REVIEW

  * The "what's included" card could never appear — it used an exact
    string match against model-written text like "Jetstar, Scoot".
  * The Flights tile counted one watch store and opened a screen
    backed by a different one. It could say "3 routes watched" and
    then show you nothing.
  * "Watch these routes" looked broken — the list filtered out
    watches with no readings yet, which is all of them at the moment
    you create them.
  * The segment sheet matched a watch from ANY trip, so last year's
    SYD-DPS showed a stale verdict against a trip six months out.
  * The flights report dropped the onward-ticket section exactly when
    the airports hadn't resolved — omitting the one thing that stops
    you boarding, in the incomplete-data case.
  * "AirAsia and AirAsia X doesn't publish fares" — duplicated
    carrier, singular verb.
  * The budget check never matched a REGION, so "Bali" — the literal
    example in its own header comment — matched nothing.
  * Dictionary iteration order made the "airline direct" button show
    a different airline on different launches.
  * The budget screen recomputed six times per render, allocating a
    NumberFormatter each time — a few thousand a second on a slider
    drag.

FOUND BY SIMULATION

  * THE ONE-WAY FLIGHT ALLOWANCE WAS A RETURN-SHAPED NUMBER. $620 per
    person as a one-way base, when Brisbane to Denpasar one-way is
    $250-400. It made every budget answer about $6 a night meaner
    than the truth — and that landed exactly on the line between
    Chiang Mai fitting and not fitting.
  * BINARY FITS / DOESN'T-FIT IS TOO BLUNT. The simulation had it
    calling Chiang Mai and Penang "SHORT" on a six per cent gap,
    which is technically true and useless as advice. Six per cent is
    a monthly rate and one fewer move, not a different country.
    There's a "tight, not wrong" band now.
